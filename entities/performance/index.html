Concept

Performance

How language is actually used in real situations, with its pauses, slips and restarts.

Performance is how language is actually used in real situations. Real speech is messy

Speakers hesitate, restart a sentence, change their plan halfway, repeat a word or make a slip, because of limited memory, distraction or a shift of attention. The knowledge stays the same; the performance changes.

Examples

  • Hesitation and restart. A speaker who knows that “She is reading a book” is correct may still say “She is… she’s reading a… a book”.
  • Change of plan. “Can you pass the… oh, never mind, I’ll get it myself.”
  • Slip of the tongue. Swapping two sounds by mistake: “a lack of pies” instead of “a pack of lies”.
  • Tiredness or speed. The same person speaks less clearly when they are tired or in a hurry.

None of these means the speaker does not know the language. Their competence is intact.

Why sociolinguists care

Real speech is exactly what sociolinguists observe. They record how people actually speak in their communities and look for the patterns in it. Dell Hymes argued that performance belongs at the centre of the study of language, not at its edge.
