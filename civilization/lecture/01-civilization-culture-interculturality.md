> How culture, civilization and interculturality differ, and how the United Kingdom took shape from 1707 to 1921.

## In short

- Culture refers more to spiritual, intellectual and artistic phenomena.
- Civilization refers more to material, technical, economic and social facts, and it is a process that keeps moving forward.
- The two concepts are interrelated, so they are often used interchangeably by mistake.
- Interculturality is the mutual exchange between cultures, with respect for each one.
- Periodization: 1707 formed Great Britain, 1801 formed the United Kingdom, 1921 brought in Northern Ireland.

## 1. Culture

**Culture**

*In plain words:* How a group lives together: the values, habits and traditions its members share.

*Teacher's definition, handout, from Latin cultura:*

> The shared values, practices, traditions, and expressions that define a group of people. It is mainly related
> to spiritual, intellectual and artistic phenomena.
>
> UNESCO defines culture as "the set of distinctive spiritual, material, intellectual and emotional features of
> society or a social group, that encompasses, not only art and literature but lifestyles, ways of living
> together, value systems, traditions and beliefs" (UNESCO, 2001).

## 2. Civilization

**Civilization**

*In plain words:* How people build and run their societies: the systems, the technology, the economy.

*Teacher's definition, handout, from Latin civis:*

> Broadly, the development of human societies and their systems (political, economic, etc.). In other words,
> civilisation refers to material, technical, economic, and social facts.

Civilization as a concept has always had an "expansive" character. Norbert Elias found that civilization
describes a process that refers to something which is constantly in motion and is constantly moving forward.

## 3. Culture or civilization?

*The two concepts, as the handout separates them*

|                | Culture                                                                                 | Civilization                                                                     |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| From Latin     | cultura                                                                                 | civis                                                                            |
| Refers more to | Spiritual, intellectual and artistic phenomena                                          | Material, technical, economic, and social facts                                  |
| Broadly        | The shared values, practices, traditions, and expressions that define a group of people | The development of human societies and their systems (political, economic, etc.) |

**Keep this:** Civilization refers more to material, technical, economic, and social facts; culture refers more to spiritual, intellectual and artistic phenomena.

## 4. Why the two get mixed up

- The two concepts are interrelated, which often leads to their misuse.
- Both are used interchangeably in the analysis of various aspects, such as religion, so they are mistakenly
  considered to represent the same notion.
- They are not distinguished clearly enough, and everyday language keeps a blurred awareness of the overlaps and
  interferences between them.

**Go deeper: So where is the line?**

The handout says one can draw a more or less distinctive line between civilization and culture: the former
refers more to material, technical, economic, and social facts, while the latter refers to spiritual,
intellectual and artistic phenomena.

## 5. Interculturality

**Interculturality**

*In plain words:* Different cultures meeting and exchanging, with each one recognized and respected.

*Teacher's definition, handout:*

> The interaction and exchange between different cultures, particularly due to the impact of colonialism,
> immigration, and globalization, has led to the emergence of multicultural societies. Interculturality, closely
> related to the concept of cultural pluralism, involves recognizing each specific culture and the need for
> comprehensive interaction and mutual exchange with others, thereby ensuring a deep understanding and respect
> for all cultural expressions.
>
> It is the mutual exchange of ideas and cultural norms, as well as the development of deep relationships. In an
> intercultural society, all members learn from one another while growing together in a peaceful manner.

**Keep this:** Interculturality is a project and a policy that multicultural societies strive to achieve peace and prevent discrimination.

## 6. Periodization: how the United Kingdom took shape

- **Pre-1707, Conflicts**

  Conflicts between England, Scotland, and Wales.

- **1707, Formation of Great Britain**

  Under the Act of Union, the formation of Great Britain with the unification of England and Wales with
  Scotland.

- **1801, Formation of the United Kingdom**

  Under the Act of Union, the formation of the United Kingdom (England, Wales, Scotland, and Ireland; later to be
  separated).

- **1921, Northern Ireland**

  The establishment of Northern Ireland, leading to the formation of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and
  Northern Ireland.

Despite their union, each state has its own identity and defining features.

- **1707**: Under the Act of Union, the formation of Great Britain: England and Wales unified with Scotland. (Handout)
- **1801**: Under the Act of Union, the formation of the United Kingdom: England, Wales, Scotland, and Ireland. (Handout)
