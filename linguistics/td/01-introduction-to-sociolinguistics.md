## 1. I. Definition

**Sociolinguistics**

*In plain words:* The scientific study of the relationship between language and society.

*Definition:*

> According to William Labov, sociolinguistics is the linguistic behavior of individuals cannot be understood
> without knowledge of the communities that they belong to.

## 2. II. Chomsky’s vision

Noam Chomsky’s vision of language rests on:

- performance and competence
- homogeneous linguistic structures

**Competence** is a speaker’s knowledge of their language.

**Example.** A speaker knows that “She is reading a book” is correct English and that “Reading she book is” is not. They
can also understand sentences they have never heard before.

**Performance** is how the language is actually used in real situations (Chomsky, 1965).

**Example.** The same speaker may say “She is… she’s reading a… a book”, with a pause, a restart and a repeated word. Or they
may swap two sounds by mistake and say “a lack of pies” instead of “a pack of lies”.

*Two ways of looking at language*

|             | Chomsky’s vision                                    | Sociolinguistics                                |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Looks at    | Competence: what a speaker knows about the language | How individuals behave linguistically           |
| Language is | Homogeneous: one uniform system                     | Tied to the communities that speakers belong to |

## 3. III. What sociolinguistics looks at

Sociolinguistics examines how people use language in different social contexts, and how social factors, cultural
backgrounds and communication situations shape their linguistic choices. It focuses on six areas:

1. **Language variation.** Differences in pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, accents and dialects among speakers
   and communities.
2. **Social factors.** How language use varies with age, social class, ethnicity, region and social identity.
3. **Social context.** How people change the way they speak with the situation, the person they address and the
   level of formality, for example to a teacher and to a close friend.
4. **Identity.** How people use language to express who they are, show that they belong to a group and build
   social relationships.
5. **Language change.** How languages change over time, and how new pronunciations, words and grammar spread
   through communities.
6. **Multilingualism and language contact.** How people use several languages or dialects, switch between them and
   adapt their speech to different groups.

**Keep this:** Language is not uniform or static: it varies with speakers, situations and communities, and it changes over time.

William Labov studies the dialects and accents that exist in New York. His research there looked at pronunciation, social
class and speaking style, and showed that linguistic variation follows systematic patterns and can be studied scientifically
in its social context.

## 4. IV. Dialect and accent

The two words are often mixed up. An **accent** is about pronunciation only. A **dialect** covers
more: vocabulary and grammar as well as pronunciation (Trudgill, 2000).

|               | Accent | Dialect |
| ------------- | ------ | ------- |
| Pronunciation | Yes    | Yes     |
| Vocabulary    | No     | Yes     |
| Grammar       | No     | Yes     |

## 5. V. Language and society

What is the possible relationship between language and society?

One common way to set out the possibilities (Wardhaugh & Fuller, 2015):

- Society influences language.
- Language influences society.
- The two influence each other.
- They are not related.

## Entities mentioned

- **William Labov** (person): American linguist (1927 to 2024) and a pioneer of variationist sociolinguistics. He studied the dialects and accents of New York.
- **Noam Chomsky** (person): American linguist, philosopher and cognitive scientist, known for generative grammar and Universal Grammar.
- **Competence** (concept): A speaker's underlying knowledge of their language, as opposed to how they use it.
- **Performance** (concept): How language is actually used in real situations, with its pauses, slips and restarts.
- **Accent** (concept): The way a speaker pronounces a language. An accent concerns pronunciation only.
- **Dialect** (concept): A variety of a language that differs in vocabulary and grammar as well as pronunciation, linked to a region or a social group.
