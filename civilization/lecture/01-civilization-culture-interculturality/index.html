Culture

Culture

In plain words

How a group lives together: the values, habits and traditions its members share.

Definition, from Latin cultura

The shared values, practices, traditions, and expressions that define a group of people. It is mainly related to spiritual, intellectual and artistic phenomena.

UNESCO defines culture as “the set of distinctive spiritual, material, intellectual and emotional features of society or a social group, that encompasses, not only art and literature but lifestyles, ways of living together, value systems, traditions and beliefs” (UNESCO, 2001).

Civilization

Civilization

In plain words

How people build and run their societies: the systems, the technology, the economy.

Definition, from Latin civis

Broadly, the development of human societies and their systems (political, economic, etc.). In other words, civilisation refers to material, technical, economic, and social facts.

Civilization as a concept has always had an “expansive” character. Culture or civilization?

Keep this

Civilization refers more to material, technical, economic, and social facts; culture refers more to spiritual, intellectual and artistic phenomena.

A way to hold the two apart: culture is what a society believes and creates; civilization is what it builds and organizes.

Why the two get mixed up

  • The two concepts are interrelated, which often leads to their misuse.

  • Both are used interchangeably in the analysis of various aspects, such as religion, so they are mistakenly considered to represent the same notion.

  • They are not distinguished clearly enough, and everyday language keeps a blurred awareness of the overlaps and interferences between them.

So where is the line?

One can draw a more or less distinctive line between civilization and culture: the former refers more to material, technical, economic, and social facts, while the latter refers to spiritual, intellectual and artistic phenomena.

Interculturality

Interculturality

In plain words

Different cultures meeting and exchanging, with each one recognized and respected.

Definition

The interaction and exchange between different cultures, particularly due to the impact of colonialism, immigration, and globalization, has led to the emergence of multicultural societies. Interculturality, closely related to the concept of cultural pluralism, involves recognizing each specific culture and the need for comprehensive interaction and mutual exchange with others, thereby ensuring a deep understanding and respect for all cultural expressions.

It is the mutual exchange of ideas and cultural norms, as well as the development of deep relationships. In an intercultural society, all members learn from one another while growing together in a peaceful manner.

Keep this

Interculturality is a project and a policy that multicultural societies strive to achieve peace and prevent discrimination.
