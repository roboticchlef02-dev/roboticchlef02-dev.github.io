Person

William Labov

American linguist (1927 to 2024) and a pioneer of variationist sociolinguistics. He studied the dialects and accents of New York.

William Labov was an American linguist and one of the pioneers of variationist sociolinguistics. His idea of the field: the linguistic behavior of individuals cannot be understood without knowledge of the communities that they belong to.

  1. 1927Born in New Jersey

    William David Labov is born on 4 December 1927 in Passaic, New Jersey, USA.

  2. 1948Harvard

    He graduates from Harvard University. He studied English and philosophy, with an additional interest in chemistry.

  3. 1949Chemist, then linguist

    He works as an industrial chemist. His daily contact with workers and everyday communication shapes his interest in ordinary people’s language. In 1961 he leaves to study linguistics at Columbia University, under Uriel Weinreich.

  4. 1960sMartha's Vineyard

    His master’s research at Columbia looks at pronunciation changes on Martha’s Vineyard, Massachusetts. It shows that language variation can be studied systematically, in its social context.

  5. 1964PhD and New York City

    He completes his PhD at Columbia. His research examines linguistic variation in New York City: the relationship between pronunciation, social class and speaking style. He is an assistant professor at Columbia from 1964 to 1970.

  6. 1966First major book

    He publishes The Social Stratification of English in New York City.

  7. 1972Sociolinguistic Patterns

    He publishes Sociolinguistic Patterns, on the relationship between linguistic variation and social factors. At the University of Pennsylvania he teaches future linguists, and his studies of speech in Harlem, New York, help show that non-standard varieties of English follow systematic grammatical rules.

  8. 2014Retires

    He retires from his university position.

  9. 2024Dies

    He dies on 17 December 2024, aged 97.

Why he matters

  • He brought systematic data collection and quantitative methods into the study of language variation. This helped make sociolinguistics a scientific field.
  • His research on African American Vernacular English showed that non-standard varieties of English have systematic grammatical rules. They should be studied scientifically and not treated as linguistically inferior.
  • His methods still shape research on language, identity, dialects and language change.
