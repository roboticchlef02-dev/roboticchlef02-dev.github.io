Noam Chomsky

American linguist, philosopher and cognitive scientist, known for generative grammar and Universal Grammar.

Avram Noam Chomsky is an American linguist, philosopher, cognitive scientist, author and political commentator. In this lesson, his vision of language rests on performanceconceptPerformanceHow language is actually used in real situations, with its pauses, slips and restarts.Read more and competenceconceptCompetenceA speaker's underlying knowledge of their language, as opposed to how they use it.Read more, and on homogeneous linguistic structures.

  1. 1928Born in Philadelphia

    He is born on 7 December 1928 in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA. From an early age he is interested in language, ideas and intellectual debates.

  2. 1949University of Pennsylvania

    He studies linguistics, philosophy and mathematics at the University of Pennsylvania: bachelor’s degree in 1949, master’s in 1951, and PhD in linguistics in 1955. The linguist Zellig Harris strongly influences him. His dissertation is Transformational Analysis. From 1951 to 1955 he is also a Junior Fellow of the Harvard Society of Fellows.

  3. 1955MIT

    He joins the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), where he works for more than six decades.

  4. 1957Syntactic Structures

    He publishes Syntactic Structures, which introduces key ideas of transformational-generative grammar: linguists should look for the underlying rules that let speakers produce and understand an unlimited number of sentences.

  5. 1965Aspects of the Theory of Syntax

    He publishes Aspects of the Theory of Syntax, which develops generative grammar and sets out the distinction between competenceconceptCompetenceA speaker's underlying knowledge of their language, as opposed to how they use it.Read more (a speaker’s underlying knowledge of language) and performanceconceptPerformanceHow language is actually used in real situations, with its pauses, slips and restarts.Read more (the actual use of language).

  6. 1960sPolitical writing

    He opposes the Vietnam War and argues that intellectuals should question political power. In 1988 he co-authors Manufacturing Consent with Edward S. Herman, on how media institutions and economic and political interests can shape the news.

  7. 1995The Minimalist Program

    He publishes The Minimalist Program, a research framework that tries to explain linguistic structure with a more economical set of principles.

  8. 2017University of Arizona

    He joins the University of Arizona as a laureate professor in the Department of Linguistics.

Why he matters

  • His generative grammar changed how linguists work: it asks which mental rules make human language possible, instead of only classifying what speakers say. His work has influenced linguistics, psychology, cognitive science, philosophy and computer science, and many of his proposals have also been debated and revised.
  • Universal Grammar is his proposal that human beings have an innate capacity for acquiring language, and that human languages share underlying principles. It is central to debates about language acquisition, cognition and the mind.
  • In sociolinguistics he is the starting point of a contrast: his vision rests on competence and on homogeneous linguistic structures, while sociolinguistics begins from the communities speakers belong to.
