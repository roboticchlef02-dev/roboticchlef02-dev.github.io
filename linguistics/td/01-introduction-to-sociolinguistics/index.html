II. Chomsky’s vision

Noam ChomskypersonNoam ChomskyAmerican linguist, philosopher and cognitive scientist, known for generative grammar and Universal Grammar.Read more’s vision of language rests on:

  • performance and competence
  • homogeneous linguistic structures

CompetenceconceptCompetenceA speaker's underlying knowledge of their language, as opposed to how they use it.Read more is a speaker’s knowledge of their language.

Example. A speaker knows that “She is reading a book” is correct English and that “Reading she book is” is not. They can also understand sentences they have never heard before.

PerformanceconceptPerformanceHow language is actually used in real situations, with its pauses, slips and restarts.Read more is how the language is actually used in real situations (Chomsky, 1965).

Example. The same speaker may say “She is… she’s reading a… a book”, with a pause, a restart and a repeated word. Or they may swap two sounds by mistake and say “a lack of pies” instead of “a pack of lies”.

III. What sociolinguistics looks at

Sociolinguistics examines how people use language in different social contexts, and how social factors, cultural backgrounds and communication situations shape their linguistic choices. It focuses on six areas:

  1. Language variation. Differences in pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, accents and dialects among speakers and communities.

  2. Social factors. How language use varies with age, social class, ethnicity, region and social identity.

  3. Social context. How people change the way they speak with the situation, the person they address and the level of formality, for example to a teacher and to a close friend.

  4. Identity. How people use language to express who they are, show that they belong to a group and build social relationships.

  5. Language change. How languages change over time, and how new pronunciations, words and grammar spread through communities.

  6. Multilingualism and language contact. How people use several languages or dialects, switch between them and adapt their speech to different groups.

Keep this

Language is not uniform or static: it varies with speakers, situations and communities, and it changes over time.

V. Language and society

What is the possible relationship between language and society?

One common way to set out the possibilities (Wardhaugh & Fuller, 2015):

  • Society influences language.
  • Language influences society.
  • The two influence each other.
  • They are not related.
